# Build brand knowledge from sources

Use this for an initial brand analysis or a refresh. For a single piece with a
sufficient brief, extract only the material needed. More files do not make an
inference more certain when they repeat the same underlying story.

## Inventory before interpretation

Identify the business, author and accounts represented. Give each supplied source
a stable ID. Record title/path or URL, publication date, observation date, speaker,
version if available, evidence form and review coverage. Keep originals intact.

Separate spoken transcript, native caption, description, slide text and visual
observation. A caption-only Reel supports a caption reading, not a full video
analysis. A sampled frame supports that frame, not a claim about the entire edit.
Record missing audio, slides, uncertain speakers and transcription errors. Search
or read the original before relying on a consequential quotation.

For a large archive, select sources by the current question, date range, author
and format. Record what was selected and why. Use focused source notes to avoid
loading the archive repeatedly; never report sampled coverage as a complete review.

## Read each source in three passes

1. **Meaning:** what happened or what is being argued, in the source's context.
   Keep chronology, disagreement and qualifications. Distinguish the author's
   position from a guest's view and a hypothetical from an actual event.
2. **Grounding:** locate passages supporting facts, experiences, offers, beliefs,
   explanations and voice observations. Capture the exact passage and location,
   then state the narrow observation it supports.
3. **Usefulness:** what customer question or decision could this help with? Note
   the practical reasoning, useful demonstration or story, not only its topic.

A compact source note contains the source ID/version, scope reviewed, main point,
selected passages and observations, candidate claims and unresolved questions.
Do not turn generated drafts back into independent source evidence.

## Assess claims before compiling the brief

Give a reusable claim a stable ID, its type, statement, supporting source locations,
qualifiers, applicable audience/platform/time period, assessment and reason.
Useful types are fact, proof, story, belief, mechanism, positioning, offer, voice
and design observation. Use only the fields needed for this business.

Keep these distinctions explicit:

- Direct owner instructions express intent; they do not prove customer outcomes.
- Observed writing habits describe the reviewed samples; they are not automatically
  mandatory rules. A deliberate edit with a stated reason carries stronger intent.
- A supported inference remains an inference. A plausible opinion on a new topic
  is not something the author has said.
- Historic, contested, superseded and unreviewed claims remain visible with those
  limits, rather than becoming current public facts.
- Several reposts, clips or quotations from one event share one evidence family.
  They do not create several independent observations of a result.

Record competing statements and their dates. A newer explicit offer correction
can retire an older offer; a newer speculative post does not silently overrule an
approved company instruction. Ask when a conflict affects the requested deliverable.

## Compile knowledge that helps a writer reason

Organize the working brand brief into overview/positioning, audience, voice,
offers/proof and design language when visual evidence exists. Each substantive
statement should lead back to its claim or source. Unknown sections stay unknown.

For audience, separate current customers, the desired audience and the buyer from
the user when relevant. Capture the situation prompting a search, the question,
the alternatives and the words customers actually use. Proposed personas are
hypotheses unless research supports them.

For a belief or mechanism, capture: the question it answers, situation in which it
holds, causal reasoning, trade-off, limits and what might change the author's view.
This helps answer new questions without repeating the same origin story. Search
for a relevant principle, then test whether its reasoning applies to the new case.
If the connection is an extrapolation, mark it as a proposed answer for confirmation.
A keyword match alone is insufficient support.

For voice, use brief excerpts showing how the person opens, explains, qualifies,
disagrees and closes. Describe the observable choice and its context. Include an
approved revision or rejected example with its reason when supplied. Do not infer
personality from a few punctuation choices or manufacture typos to sound human.

Keep platform tactics, series structures and content lengths out of universal
voice. The author may write formal product announcements and informal captions.
For design, describe visible type, hierarchy, imagery and spacing only where the
visuals were actually inspected; captions cannot establish a visual identity.

## Refresh without losing the reasoning

When material changes, identify the source revision or owner correction and the
claims it affects. Mark affected knowledge stale until checked. Preserve prior
wording, date and the reason for the change in the existing brief's history; do not
rewrite unrelated accepted sections. A fresh export of old material is not fresh
verification. Recheck current offers and product availability before public use.

Identify plan slots, piece briefs and review states depending on a changed claim,
offer or asset. Recheck those dependencies and update their current status while
preserving dated prior reviews. Finishing a promised resource does not establish
that delivery works; copy may remain complete with fulfilment blocked until the
route and access are checked. Do not rewrite unrelated copy merely because brand
knowledge has a newer revision.
