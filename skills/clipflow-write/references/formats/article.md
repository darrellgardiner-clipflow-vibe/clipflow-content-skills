# Articles, teaching guides and first-person essays

Choose the reader's question and the supported answer before deciding length.
Distinguish a sustained explanation, a first-person decision essay, a source-based
adaptation and a progress report. Each has a different reason to exist.

## Explanation or guide

Write the section headings first, in the order the reader needs the answer.
Prefer headings that state the useful point or name a concrete step. Read them
alone to check progression. Introduce the answer early rather than delaying it
behind a long industry preamble.

Develop each section through a clear point, explanation and relevant example or
procedure. Include prerequisites and observable outcomes for instructions. Lists
are useful for parallel options or steps; retain connected prose where the logic
depends on one sentence leading to the next. Vary the size of paragraphs and
sections rather than repeating one template throughout.

## First-person decision essay

Use one real decision, constraint or lesson. Locate the event, explain the trade-off
and the author's reasoning, and describe only the consequence actually known.
An unresolved consequence is a valid ending. Avoid adding a dramatic setback,
confession or triumph to complete a story shape.

Use the author's concrete language and perspective. Technical detail belongs when
it explains the choice. End with the actual implication, next decision or example;
a forced universal lesson can erase what makes the experience useful. One source
may support several essays when each develops a distinct decision.

## Source-based adaptation or progress report

Select a reader-relevant lesson from the source; avoid narrating the recording
from start to finish. Keep who said what, source date and qualifications intact.
A third-party claim remains attributed even when the wording is paraphrased.

For progress reports, distinguish shipped work, tests and plans; verify dated
product claims before turning them into instructions. Preserve historical context
instead of inserting today's capabilities into an older account. Link current
help or product proof when relevant and available. Source receipts and internal
readiness notes stay outside the article, while material limitations stay visible.

## Title, ending and production handoff

Write a title that matches the actual answer and sounds natural for the author.
Specific language and a clear stance are usually more useful than a mechanically
complete summary. An elliptical title needs its opening to supply context.
Preserve an existing public slug unless changing it is in scope.

Finish when the question is answered. Remove repeated conclusions and unrelated
product mentions. For publication, deliver requested metadata, verified source
links, quote/claim receipts and image needs. A cover brief follows the article's
point and available authentic assets; it is separate from finished artwork.
Embedding, CMS publication and live-page checks use the publishing workflow.
