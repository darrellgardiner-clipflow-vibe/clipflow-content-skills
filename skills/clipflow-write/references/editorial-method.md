# Interpret, select and structure a piece

Use this before writing or when a draft's argument is failing. For a narrow edit,
apply only the decisions affected by the request; preserve the author's meaning,
wording and existing argument outside that scope.

## Choose the work mode

- **Create:** turn a brief and evidence into an angle, structure and draft.
- **Edit:** solve the stated problem in the supplied draft with the smallest useful change.
- **Adapt:** retain meaning and attribution while changing the structure for a destination.
- **Analyse:** explain a source's components and reusable method without inventing a draft request.
- **Evaluate:** assess the actual draft and propose or apply the requested repairs.

Read for meaning, grounding and relevance. Record the reader's question in their
language, the supported answer in one sentence, and its boundary. If qualification
makes the proposed opening untrue, change the opening. Identify the business goal
without forcing every educational piece to become a product pitch.

## Match the job to the reader's situation

| Reader situation | Useful job | Support needed |
|---|---|---|
| Has not recognized a problem | Show a familiar situation and consequence | Real scene or clearly illustrative example |
| Recognizes it but is stuck | Explain a cause or achievable next step | Mechanism, demonstration or bounded reasoning |
| Comparing approaches | Help choose by meaningful criteria | Comparable evidence and trade-offs |
| Considering this offer | Demonstrate fit and answer an objection | Current offer terms and relevant proof |
| Ready to act | Make the next action clear | Available deliverable and working destination |
| Existing customer needs help | Resolve the task | Correct prerequisites, steps and expected outcome |

Treat awareness as a working interpretation from the brief, not a personality
label. If uncertain, avoid assuming the reader already knows the product.

## Select an angle with an actual payoff

For a simple requested piece, use the supplied angle. When the source supports
several interpretations, compare a few by reader payoff, strongest evidence,
author fit, production needs and risk. An angle is a specific answer or argument;
a topic such as "customer service" is too broad to draft from.

Reject angles requiring invented results, personal opinions or unavailable visual
proof. Prefer the strongest useful supported answer over novelty alone. Choose
a [pattern](patterns.md) only when it expresses the material naturally.

## Write the skeleton before the prose

List the question, chosen answer, opening promise and the job of each section,
slide or spoken beat. Attach source/claim IDs to consequential points. Read the
sequence alone: does each part advance the answer, and does the order explain why
one follows the previous? Remove interesting tangents or give them another piece.

For articles, use specific subheads that carry the argument. For carousels use
slide headlines; for video use a private beat/chapter outline. These notes are not
spoken dialogue. Existing author drafts do not need a new visible heading system
unless the edit warrants one.

Open a section with its useful point, develop it through evidence and explanation,
then move on when its work is done. Let paragraph and sentence lengths vary with
the reasoning. Use bullets for items that can be reordered, numbered steps for a
sequence, and connected prose for events or arguments whose order matters. Avoid
forcing every section into the same paragraph count or a fixed rhythm formula.

## Repurpose a source into distinct pieces

Inventory its decisions, useful questions, mechanisms, stories, demonstrations,
proof and quotable lines, with source locations. Select only extracts with a
standalone payoff. Keep a shared claim ledger so dates, numbers and qualifiers
stay consistent across assets.

Choose the destination based on how that extract works: a visible demonstration
may suit video, a sequence worth rereading may suit a carousel, and a sustained
argument may suit an article. Different posts need distinct reader jobs or angles;
changing the hook alone does not create several useful pieces.

Reconstruct context for someone who never saw the original. Adapt the opening,
evidence density and ending to the destination. Retain attribution wherever its
removal would make another person's claim sound like this author's experience.
Use existing footage for a selected moment when available; cite the actual times,
then hand editing to the production workflow. Do not manufacture clip timings.

## Resolved brief

In the existing piece record, keep mode, reader/situation, question, supported
answer and boundary, purpose, author, destination, pattern, selected evidence,
outline, visual needs, CTA or `none`, preserve list and unresolved dependencies.
Add the selected rule sources and exceptions from [voice and rules](voice-and-rules.md).
A short piece may need only a few lines; this is a decision record, not a form to
fill with guesses.
