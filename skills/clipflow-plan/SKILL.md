# Plan a week they can actually make

A good plan is one the owner finishes. It uses the recordings they already have,
fits the hours they've got, and gives each post a useful job that serves the
business's goal: answering a buying question, showing how they work, building
trust or helping an existing customer.

Read `content/brief.md`, `content/ideas.md` and any earlier `week-plan.md`. If
there's no brief, run [clipflow-brand](../clipflow-brand/SKILL.md) first. Use
the templates in [files](../clipflow-content/references/files.md) and follow the
[ground rules](../clipflow-content/references/ground-rules.md).

## 1. Keep or set the posting rhythm

Use [cadence and recurring posts](references/cadence.md) to keep an existing
routine or propose one that fits the business. Record the regular jobs, frequency
and channels in the brief. A slot is a recurring audience job; this week's idea
fills it. Keep approved commitments and distinguish one busy week's exception
from an agreed change to the routine.

## 2. Mine ideas from the material

Go back through the transcripts with [finding ideas](references/finding-ideas.md)
and add supported ideas to `content/ideas.md`. Aim for 10 to 15 when the material
supports it; keep fewer when it does not. Read earlier ideas first, including
unused material, unfinished posts and older pieces worth updating. Write each
as a hook, not a topic:
"Why your neck hurts more on Fridays", not "Neck pain". Tag each with where it
comes from, down to the timestamps.

Look hardest for moments you can clip as they are: 20 to 60 seconds where they
answer one question or tell one story, and it makes sense if it's the first
thing a stranger hears. A good clip means the owner doesn't film anything new.

## 3. Pick the week

Fill the agreed recurring posts from the supported ideas. If no cadence or
count is known, three posts is a provisional starting point only when their
material and time support it. Give each piece a job:

| Job | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Brings enquiries** | Answers a question people ask right before buying, and ends with a direct ask | "How much does a kitchen reno actually cost in Brisbane?" Book a quote |
| **Builds trust** | A real story, a mistake, a result, how they actually work | The job that went wrong and what they changed |

Use the business's goal when choosing the jobs; useful education or customer
support may matter more than a direct enquiry for a particular piece. An easy
clip is a production shortcut, not an audience job of its own.

Flag a recurring post with no source or unavailable proof. Choose a suitable
supported backup, propose a one-week change, or leave it open with a sourcing
task. Don't fill a gap with generic tips or an invented result.

One post can do two jobs. Avoid repeating the same story on one channel just
to fill the week. A useful adaptation for another channel can share its source;
label the reuse and count its extra work. Format follows the material:

- Someone talking well on camera: clip it, or a short talking video (Reel, TikTok, Short).
- Steps, a list, a before and after, a comparison: carousel.
- An opinion with a reason, aimed at other professionals: text post (LinkedIn, Facebook).

Post where they already post. Don't add platforms they didn't mention.

## 4. Fit it to their time

Give each post a time estimate and say they're estimates:

| Post | Rough time |
|---|---|
| Clip from an existing recording, with captions | 20 to 30 min |
| New talking video, one take, script provided | 30 to 60 min including setup |
| Carousel from existing material | 30 to 45 min |
| Text post | 10 to 15 min |

These ranges are rough starting points, not promises. Include the remaining
work to find material, write, film, edit/design, review and prepare the post.
Note what is already finished and what is outside an estimate.

If the week doesn't fit their time, propose reducing or deferring a post while
preserving approved work. A plan of two posts that get made beats five that
don't. Batch new filming into one session where you can.

## 5. Write the plan

Write `content/week-plan.md` with [the template](../clipflow-content/references/files.md):
the table, then each post's hook, why it's in the week, what it's made from,
the concrete to-do and the ask when useful. Link each piece to its regular post
or mark it as a one-off. Give checked trim points for clips. Mark selected ideas
as planned in `ideas.md`; update produced/published only when actually known.

Add two or three supported backups when available, and a **Next time you film**
list of useful questions for their next recording session. Pick them to fill
the brief's gaps (no reviews,
no prices, no stories about X) and to give next week its posts.

Review earlier planned, produced and published work, including what was missed
and why. If actual results exist in `content/results.md`, let them inform a
scoped next test. Check source supply and workload before changing the recurring
rhythm; keep a familiar voice and useful series without repeating the same post.
Planning alone does not schedule anything in Clipflow.

## Hand back

If planning was the last step, give the handover from
[clipflow-content](../clipflow-content/SKILL.md): the week in one line per post
and what they need to do. If writing was requested, continue to
[clipflow-write](../clipflow-write/SKILL.md) for every supported piece in the week. Leave unresolved recurring posts as
sourcing tasks rather than inventing copy to fill them.
