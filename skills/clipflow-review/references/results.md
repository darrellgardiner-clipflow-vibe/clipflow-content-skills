# Reading post results

Use what they share: screenshots of insights, an export, or numbers typed into
chat. If they share nothing, don't guess how posts did.

## What to record

Add a row per post to `content/results.md`. Lead with what matters to the
business, which is rarely views: enquiries, DMs, bookings, calls, link clicks,
saves, shares, follows. Ask what they count as a good result if you don't know.

Record enquiries or bookings they tell you came from a post, even if no app
shows it ("two people mentioned the Friday neck reel when they booked").

## Comparing fairly

- Compare a post with their own posts in the same format, not with other
  accounts.
- Only say a post did better or worse than usual when there are at least five
  similar posts to compare with. Use the middle value of those, not the
  average, so one big post doesn't skew it.
- Note when posts aren't comparable: one was boosted, one has been up for a
  month and another for two days, one was a collab.
- Followers, views and likes measure different things. Don't treat one as the
  other.

## What to tell them

One plain sentence per post ("The Friday neck reel brought two DMs, more than
any post this month"). Then:

- **Likely reasons**, said as likely, not proven: the hook was a customer's
  question, it was a clip of them explaining, it went out on a weekday morning.
- **One thing to try next week**, specific enough to do: "Next week, open two
  posts with a question a patient asked you."

Write "What stood out" and "Try next week" into `results.md`, and use them when
planning the next week. A pattern from a few posts is something to test again,
not a rule for the brief.
