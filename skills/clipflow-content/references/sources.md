# Manual and connected evidence

Use the smallest sufficient input. A missing connector does not block work from supplied files. Preserve privacy and the requested identity scope. Do not move private source material into a public package.

## Manual

Accept approved brand documents, examples of accepted/rejected copy, transcripts, customer language, product/offer facts, available assets and analytics exports. Record who supplied them and which statements are approved versus inferred. If only one sample exists, describe that narrow coverage instead of inventing a stable voice.

Read an existing brief first. Ask only for missing essentials; an unknown revenue figure does not prevent a piece that makes no revenue claim. Do not browse or purchase research merely to fill optional fields.

## Brand Lens as a skill workflow

Use [clipflow-brand](../../clipflow-brand/SKILL.md) and its
[extraction method](../../clipflow-brand/references/extraction.md) to perform brand
analysis from project files. Read supplied posts, transcripts, owner instructions and
existing briefs; extract grounded observations and compile the relevant audience,
positioning, voice, offers and proof into the shared working record.

Keep direct owner instructions separate from observed patterns and inferred
views. A folder of posts is not necessarily a reviewed voice profile. Preserve
source locations, dates, coverage and qualifiers. An old offer stays historical
when the owner supplies a newer correction. Refresh only affected fields and
record why they changed, without inventing approval for inferred statements.

No separate Brand Lens app, API, database or service is required. If the user
supplies an existing brand export, treat it as dated input with its provenance,
not a reason to demand access to the service that produced it.

## Clipflow

Clipflow is the optional connector for account context and requested workspace,
scheduling or publishing actions. Read [the Clipflow handoff](clipflow.md) when
using it. A missing connection does not block local brand analysis, planning,
writing or review. Do not invent tools or assume every account has the same
capabilities.

## Other-account research

Accept supplied post examples or research exports as reference material.
Keep them separate from this brand's own experience and proof.

Useful fields: account, post URL/ID, publication and observation dates, format, original hook and available transcript/visual evidence, views, recent comparable-post median, baseline sample size/window, paid/pinned status when known, and missing-data notes. Preserve each row's source and metric units.

Compare views with the same account's recent comparable-post median only when that baseline is supplied or can be calculated from the actual sample. Keep follower-based ratios separate; never label a mixed batch as median-based. Unknown baselines remain unknown. Compare post ages and promotion conditions, and report small/selected samples as limited evidence. Do not invent retention, causal effects or winning formulas from view counts.

Borrow the mechanism or reader question, then answer it using this brand's own evidence. Do not copy another account's script or import its results as first-person proof.

## Hook collections

Select a few relevant patterns by question, format and evidence. A template's number, outcome or opinion must come from the current speaker's evidence. Historical "viral" labels and collected performance claims are unverified unless checked against sources. Do not load thousands of examples by default or require a numerical hook score.
