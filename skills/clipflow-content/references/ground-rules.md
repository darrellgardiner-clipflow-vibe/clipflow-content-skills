# Ground rules

These keep the posts true and keep the owner out of trouble. They apply to every
skill in the pack.

## Only what they gave you

- Every fact, number, price, result, story and credential in a post comes from
  their material or from what they told you. A post that needs a testimonial,
  a client result or a statistic you don't have gets a different angle, or a
  clear `[ADD: a real client result]` gap in the notes, never a made-up one.
- An opinion on a new topic is not something they've said. If a post needs
  their take and you only have a guess, write the guess in the notes as a
  question for them, not into the copy.
- A guest's or interviewer's view isn't the owner's. Check who's speaking.
- The same story told in three videos is still one story. Don't count it as
  three pieces of proof or repeat it on one channel just to fill the week.
  When adapting it for another channel, give that version a useful purpose and
  mark the shared source rather than calling it a new result.
- Old offers and prices stay out when they've told you something newer. If
  you're not sure an offer is current, ask before it goes in a post.
- Other accounts' posts can give you a format or a question to answer. Their
  results, stories and numbers never become the owner's.

## Guesses are labelled

When you have to assume something (the audience, the voice from one sample, a
time estimate), say so in the brief or the plan. Thin material is fine; pretending
it's thick isn't. One sample means the voice section is a first guess.

## Their voice wins

Their own phrases, quirks and opinions beat tidy copy. Keep a call to action
they asked for, even if it sounds ordinary, and check the link, word or offer
behind it exists. If it doesn't yet, the post can still be written; flag it as
the thing to fix before posting.

## Privacy

- Don't upload recordings or documents to any other service without asking.
- Don't install software without asking.
- Leave customers' names, faces, health details and anything identifying out of
  public copy unless the owner says that customer agreed. Never name children;
  use "my son" or "a client's daughter".
- Keep each business separate. An agency or freelancer with several clients
  gets one `content/` folder per client.

## Nothing goes out on its own

These skills write files. They don't post, schedule, email or message anyone.
If the owner asks to save or schedule in Clipflow, follow
[the Clipflow connector](clipflow.md). Asking for a plan or a review is not
permission to publish.

## What you can't promise

Don't predict views or promise growth. Don't claim a post will "go viral". A
review can say what looks strong or weak and why, and suggest something to test.
