# Fictional business: Orbit Studio

These are invented source records for a standalone skill exercise, not a case study.

## Request

Build the relevant brand knowledge and adapt the source into a short founder
article and one customer newsletter. Then separately edit the existing social
caption only to correct outdated availability. Keep the author's other wording.
Write locally, without research or connected tools. No sending or publishing.

## S1: founder interview, 7 November 2026

Jules: "We design onboarding materials for small software teams. A team gave us
three different explanations of the same setup step. I put the three drafts side
by side and asked who the reader was meant to be. One was for a developer, one
was for an administrator and one was for a first-time user. None was the wrong
answer, but they couldn't be the same page. We split them by the job each person
needed to finish. We haven't measured support volume after the change."

Jules: "My view is that we should choose the user's task before choosing how much
to write. I don't think every guide should be short. Sometimes the missing detail
is the reason a user gets stuck. I want people to be able to follow the reasoning."

## S2: approved author sample

"The short version left out the permission step. So we put it back. The page got
longer and the instructions finally described what the customer had to do."

Owner instruction: first person is for Jules's actual decisions; company service
emails use "we". A piece can be useful without a sales CTA. Avoid inventing doubt
or vulnerability, and don't turn every paragraph into a single-line slogan.
